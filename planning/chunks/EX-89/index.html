    EX-89 (imp:7): why a lead's tip response cannot be read off the lead-free field. One mesh (182 371 cells; 15 000 lead cells, 7 605 tip-ball cells) and one σ = 800 S/m capped rod (r = 1 mm, tip to tip 32 mm, centre (15, 0, 0) mm) in the coil-driven gel phantom at 64 MHz: - One adjoint solve gives the TF. A unit tip load (the mean E_z in a 3 mm ball at the top tip) solved with the lead present gives w, and TF(z) = −jωμ₀Δσ·w_z along the lead. Folded with each port drive's lead-free field it predicts the with-lead tip response exactly (T3), and as a 1-D path integral ∫TF(z)·E_z(z) dz within the 5 % band (PF). - The new angle: a drive the adjoint never saw. The clinical quadrature drive (all four ports at once, phases from the fixture's own azimuths) is solved fresh with the lead. Folding the same w with the superposed lead-free field predicts its tip response at DISCRETE_IDENTITY_RTOL. The TF is a property of the lead in this coil and phantom, not of the drive. - Why the lead-free field is not enough. The Born fold (the adjoint solved without the lead) overstates the response ≈ 14×, because the lead excludes the axial field from its own interior (quasi-static depolarisation ≈ 0.07 at aspect 16, WF-9.md item 224). The fold of w with the with-lead field (C2) captures only ≈ 6.5 %.
