    EX-87 (imp:5): a lead shaft in the gel. The wire shorts the field that runs along it and doubles the field that crosses it. One mesh and one 1 mm PEC shaft in IMP-1's host (σ 0.57 S/m, εᵣ 78, 64 MHz) are driven two ways: - E along the shaft (TM_z, E_z). The surrounding gel goes cold: the annulus a < ρ < 1.5a carries Φ ≈ 3.7e-3, so SAR there is ≈ 0.4 % of the open-gel SAR at E₀. The drive's energy turns into the axial current I ≈ 2.9 mA per V/m. On a finite lead, that current is what imp:2 – imp:4 find at the tip. - E across the shaft (TE_z, E_⊥). The field doubles at the two points facing it, so the surface SAR there is ≈ 4× open gel, and falls to zero on the two flanks. The annulus mean is Φ ≈ 1.44. - The finite-k angle. The TE near field is quasi-static to ≈ 2e-4. The TM field has no quasi-static limit: as |k|a → 0 it falls only as 1/ln(1/|k|a). A 10× thinner wire still carries ≈ 65 % of the current. - The fixture angle. A 3D slab stands in for the infinite wire only if each polarisation gets its own end condition. With the end conditions swapped, the field never reaches the shaft.
