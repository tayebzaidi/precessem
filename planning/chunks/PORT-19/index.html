    PORT-19 — Reuse the factorisation across the drives of one sweep¶

    Reuse the factorisation across the drives of one sweep — one factorisation per sweep instead of one per port. Opened 2026-09-10, operator direction, on measured cost. The premise, verified in code before this was scoped: in run_lumped_sheet_port_case the extra_bilinear_terms list is built over every port id and references only sheet.sheet_resistivity and ω (ports/lumped.py:484–489), while extra_linear_terms references only sheets[driven_port_id] (:490–493). The system matrix is therefore the same for every drive of a matched-termination sweep and only the right-hand side changes — the textbook multiple-RHS case. Today each drive builds its own LinearProblem and re-factorises: the ANS-4 step 2d log shows one factorize+solve starting per drive, fifteen for fifteen drives. The cost, measured on that run (-n 16, degree 2, 128 MHz): 743 k unknowns 42 s/drive, 1.80 M unknowns 280 s/drive, 3.83 M unknowns 1335 s/drive — and the factorisation is almost all of it. Consequence for the deliverable: a 32-port human-scale sweep at the resolution ANS-4 matched AED at is ≈ 2.5 M unknowns × 32 drives ≈ 5.4 h, which fits no window; with reuse it is one factorisation plus 32 back-substitutions, ≈ 20 min. That is worth more than the 512 → 754 GiB tier and more than extra cores, and it is why xxl is a safety net rather than the fix. Step 1 — prove the premise by measurement, not by reading (smoke, -n 2, no new solver path): assemble the sweep's matrix for drive 1 and for drive 2 on the gated 4-leg fixture and assert they are bit-identical — same nonzero pattern, and ‖A₁ − A₂‖_∞ == 0.0 exactly, not to a tolerance — while the two right-hand sides differ (‖b₁ − b₂‖₂ > 0; the earlier wording "by more than the reference impedance" compared a vector norm to ohms and was struck 2026-09-11 03:00 review). If they are not identical the chunk is void and the finding is the negative result: say which term carries the drive and stop. Step 2 — implement (standard, -n 2): factor once per sweep and solve N right-hand sides, in ports/sparameters.py, behind a default-on keyword so the per-drive path stays reachable for a control. dolfinx's LinearProblem discards its factor with the object, so the reuse has to hold the PETSc KSP/PC across solves — core/time_harmonic.py:563–579 already documents that holding it holds the MUMPS factor and names the peak-memory tradeoff, which here is cheaper than today because one factor is held instead of N built in sequence. Gates, every band imported and unmoved: the 4-leg 4×4 at 10 MHz reproduces PORT-9 leg (d)'s recorded S to 1e-12 or better (it is the same arithmetic, so anything looser is a defect, not a tolerance); reciprocity, σ_max and the C4 class spreads unchanged; and the per-drive route re-run in the same window agrees with the reused route entry by entry. Negative control, asserted: with reuse forced on a sweep whose drives do change the matrix (perturb one port's port_impedance_ohm), the reused result must differ from the per-drive result by ≥ 100× the reproduction band — i.e. the optimisation is provably unsafe exactly where its premise fails, so a future change that breaks the premise cannot pass silently. Printed, gated by nothing: wall time per drive before and after, and the factor's memory. Done-when: both steps ✅, the 32-port PORT-13 sweep re-run and its recorded 32×32 reproduced, the speedup recorded as a measurement rather than a claim. Not in scope: iterative solvers, preconditioners, and any change to the port model — this chunk changes when a factorisation happens and nothing else.
