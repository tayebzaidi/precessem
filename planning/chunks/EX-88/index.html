    EX-88 (imp:6): an insulated lead shaft in the gel. The sleeve takes the field that crosses the wire and moves its hot spot 90°; the field that runs along the wire does not notice it. One mesh (25 878 cells: host 16 255, sleeve 9 623) and one 1 mm PEC shaft with a 0.5 mm εᵣ 3 lossless sleeve, in IMP-1's host (σ 0.57 S/m, εᵣ 78, 64 MHz): - E across the shaft (TE_z). Normal D is continuous at ρ = b, so the normal E jumps by |ε_c,host/ε_c,coat| ≈ 59 (59.36 at finite k, 20261004T003556Z_IMP-5-step0-W1b.log:115). The field crowds into the insulation: |E| at the conductor surface inside the sleeve is ≈ 7.06·E₀ (…W1b.log:122, |E_ρ| max 7.056), against 2.00·E₀ on a bare wire, and the sleeve mean ⟨|E|²⟩/E₀² is 17.99 against the bare wire's 1.445 over the same annulus. The gel just outside sees its normal field suppressed (|E_ρ(b⁺)| ≈ 0.086·E₀) and its tangential field nearly doubled (|E_φ(b)| ≈ 1.96·E₀): the near-lead hot spot moves from the two facing points (bare wire) to the two flanks, and the host-annulus SAR b < ρ < 1.5b rises ≈ 30 % (1.411 against 1.088, …W1b.log:116). The induced dipole reverses (t/t₀ ≈ −2.16, :114). - E along the shaft (TM_z). A non-magnetic sleeve is invisible at leading order. The scattering coefficient moves by |s/s₀ − 1| = 3.1e-6 and the wire current by 4.3e-5 (:113). The FEM's own TM |β − 1| is 3.1e-4 (20261004T035027Z_IMP-5-step1-W1a.log:146), 7× the current shift and 100× the s shift, so the FEM cannot see the coating in TM, and the TM gate is not a coating test. The guide says so plainly, and also says this is a k_z = 0 statement: on a real insulated lead, k_z ≠ 0 propagation is where insulation sets lead heating, and that is not this fixture. - The same-mesh angle. A bare wire solved on the same mesh (sleeve cells set to gel, IMP-4's exact bare trace) gives the per-cell SAR gain of insulation directly in ParaView.
