    Step 1b-0 — the dipole / sphere-reflection anchor, numpy only (§9 item 107, 2026-09-30 12:00 slot)¶

    Why a sphere, not the box (10:30 review, journal docs/planning/reviews/2026-09-30-daily-1030.md §4). The entry's original step-1b clause cannot produce a band, for two reasons:

    1. R(θ) is planar. Jin (9.48) is the reflection of a plane wave off a flat first-order ABC. Its rms over a cube, weighted by the dipole's power pattern sin²θ_z, is ρ ≈ 0.113 whatever the box size (measured here: 0.11309, max |R| 0.26795 in the corner direction). No box size makes that number smaller, so it cannot set a band that tightens as the box grows.
    2. Curvature is missing. On a curved boundary the first-order condition also reflects through the 1/(2(k₀R)²) term: 5.1 % at 0.5 λ₀. A box has no closed-form reflection. A sphere has one.

    The anchor. Take a z-directed Hertzian dipole with e^{+jωt}, x = k₀r, C = 1, f = 1 − j/x − 1/x² and g = 1 − j/x:

    • E_r = 2C cosθ (1/x² − j/x³) e^{−jx}
    • E_θ = C sinθ (j f/x) e^{−jx}
    • η₀H_φ = C sinθ (j g/x) e^{−jx}

    These are the standard small-dipole fields (Balanis, Antenna Theory, eqs. 4-8 / 4-10). That citation is from memory and not verified in-repo; the converted Jin text does not carry the dipole. For that reason (n1) and (n2) verify the field independently.

    On r = R the first-order ABC n × ∇ × E + jk₀ n × n × E = 0 (Jin §9.2 (9.44) at s = 1, §9.3 (9.54)) reduces to E_θ = η₀H_φ. Write the field as E = A·E_out + B·conj(E_out); the incoming wave's field is H_in = −conj(H_out). For a general surface impedance E_θ = z η₀ H_φ this gives

    Γ(z) = B/A = (f − z g) e^{−2jx} / (f̄ + z ḡ)

    and four special cases:

    • z = 1 (the ABC): Γ_sph = −e^{−2jx}/(2x² − 1 + 2jx), with |Γ_sph| = 1/√(1 + 4x⁴).
    • z = f/g (the exact impedance): Γ = 0.
    • z = 0 (PEC): Γ = (f/f̄) e^{−2jx}, with |Γ| = 1.
    • z = −1 (γ → −γ): |Γ| = √(1 + 4x⁴).

    The ABC reflects a fraction 1/(2x²) of the wave for x ≫ 1, and fully as x → 0.
