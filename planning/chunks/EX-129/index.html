    Angle (the drive). P36 heats the 650 × 420 × 90 mm box with q = 3Q(y² + z²)/(B² + C²) = Q[w_y(y/B)² + w_z(z/C)²], where w_y = 2.868293 and w_z = 0.131707. This is the transverse SAR shape of a long body in a circularly polarised B₁: E_x = jω(B_z y − B_y z), so |E_x|² ∝ y² + z² (MAT-21.md:1403–1409). In the F2182 placement x is the bore axis (GEO-38.md:354). - The weights are physics, not tuning (10:30 ruling, MAT-21.md:1658). - The mean source is exactly Q, so the source power is the same 49.14 W as in mat:19 / mat:20.

    Four things are new to the examples: 1. A source that varies along the two transverse axes. The hot spot sits next to the long ±y side walls, not at the centre (mat:19) or the ±x ends (mat:20). 2. The y centre line. The P2 solve is plotted against the quadratic series and the uniform-source run. 3. The face-heat split moves toward ±y, the opposite pair from mat:20. 4. The thin-axis term in ParaView. The z² term carries 4.4 % of the power on the 90 mm axis. The gate's (c-z) shows the solve resolves it, and the example shows where it sits (theta_quad_minus_noz_series).

    Printed only: the centre-versus-side ratio at t* against steady state. This is a probe-placement reading, with no compliance claim.
