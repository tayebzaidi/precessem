`EX-90` (`mat:13`): **what a temperature probe in a heated gel ball reports, and why its stated volume matters.** One EM solve on `MAT-4`'s lossy sphere (R = 10 mm, σ 0.57 S/m, 64 MHz, imposed field) heats the 19 496-cell sphere submesh. Robin h = 100 W/m²/K, CN + Rannacher, N = 40 to t\* = 139.33 s. The run is `MAT-12`'s, and the probe is `MAT-19`'s:
- **What the probe reads.** Three probes of stated radius a = 2 mm (centres 0, 3.5 mm on x, 6.5 mm off-axis) each give a ball-averaged series of 41 points. Each is set beside two comparands: the **exact ball mean** θ̄(d, a, t) of the Robin eigen-series, which the gate asserts within 1 %, and the **point value** θ(d, t), which a bare comparison would use.
- **The new angle: the probe volume.** At c₀ the same run is read again with a = 1, 4 and 8 mm. The volume offset (ball − point) grows as a². In the steady limit it is exactly −q a²/(10k), because θ_ss is quadratic in r and the ball mean of \|x\|² is d² + 3a²/5. At a = 2 mm it sits *below* the 1 % band at c₀ (−0.73 % at t\*), so the gate cannot see it there. At a = 8 mm it is −13.4 %, and a point comparand misses by ≈ 13× the band (C3).
- **Position beats volume at this a/R.** A 1 mm placement error at 6.5 mm (C1, 5.42 % at t\*) costs ≈ 4× the a = 2 mm volume offset there (−1.26 % exact, −1.35 % FEM).
