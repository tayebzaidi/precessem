**The angle: a gel-phantom exposure story.** The 10 cm Robin gel ball of `MAT-9` / `MAT-17` is switched 0/1 with P = 1200 s and D = 0.5 (case A). The example asks what two regulatory post-processors report on this history, and checks each against a closed form:
1. **The six-minute average of the power.** The source schedule χ(t) is on for 600 s, and 600 s exceeds the 360 s window. The six-minute mean of χ therefore peaks at **1, full power**, while its whole-period mean is exactly **D = 0.5**. A six-minute SAR-style average reads twice the period average on this schedule.
2. **The six-minute average of the temperature.** The thermal mass (τ₁ = 1.9155e4 s ≫ P) flattens this. The six-minute mean of θ at the centre peaks only ≈ +0.8 % above D·θ_ss(0), at t ≈ 780 s (`MAT-20` (B2)). A window of a whole number of periods sees D·θ_ss exactly ((B1)).
3. **CEM43 of the same history.** The temperatures are re-based to 37 °C, as `MAT-20` (A2) did: θ is unchanged and only the additive base moves. The centre's periodic state then sits at 43.04–43.18 °C. Because 2^(T−43) is convex, the duty-cycled history's dose per period exceeds that of the average-power stand-in (θ ≡ D·θ_ss) by ≈ 4e-4 relative (Jensen). That holds only where T stays on one side of 43 °C. CEM43's R switch makes the integrand **concave** at 43 °C (slope 2 ln 2 below against ln 2 above), so the reading flips sign near the mean-43 °C isotherm (r ≈ 20 mm). The map shows this.
4. **The same dose for a bench gel.** On a 20 °C base the centre dose per period is ≈ 1e-7 s. CEM43 is a tissue metric. The 37 °C base is the gate's re-basing, not a claim about any phantom.
