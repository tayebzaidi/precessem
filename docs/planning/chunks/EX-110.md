**The angle: the regulatory window on a real FEM history, and on a field.** `mat:17` showed the six-minute window on closed forms. `mat:15` showed this coil-driven duty history without a window. This example joins them and adds three things no example has:
1. **Windowing commutes with Duhamel on FEM data.** The duty run's six-minute mean equals the signed sum of the ten switch-on runs' six-minute means ((FEM), gated). So a user can window the per-switch building blocks once and compose any on/off schedule on the same switch grid without re-windowing. The guide states this as the reason; the example asserts only the gated schedule.
2. **The window of a field.** The 2-D call windows θ at every P1 vertex of the phantom. The example writes θ and its six-minute mean as one ParaView time series (29 frames, n = 12 … 40). It also writes, at t\*, the per-vertex six-minute maximum and **the time at which each vertex's six-minute mean peaks**. The volume mean peaks at t\* (record `:1974`). But a window ending at the last on-phase end (n = 36, t = 1128.6 s) holds 234.6 s of on-time, against 125.4 s for the window ending at t\* (n = 40). So vertices whose θ falls during the off phase may peak earlier. The map shows where.
3. **Power against temperature.** The source's six-minute on-fraction peaks at (P/2 + W − P)/W = 234.6/360 = 0.6517, which is 1.303 × D: plain arithmetic on `HALF` and `W`, printed. The volume-mean θ's six-minute peak is 0.5270 K against H_0's 1.0044 K, a ratio of 0.5247 ≈ D (`:1974`). The thermal mass flattens what the power window reports.
